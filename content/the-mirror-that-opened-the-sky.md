## What if the first telescope began as an imperfect mirror?

Step into an Egyptian workshop. A polished surface curves almost imperceptibly.
Someone steps back—and sees something unexpected.

From that small departure, a different history of science unfolds. How might
craftspeople turn a mirror into an instrument? How could a swinging weight keep
time, a measured stretch of Earth preserve a unit of length, and a flash of
sunlight reveal the scale of the sky?

This book invites you to build the experiment one problem at a time. Each
obstacle demands another invention. Each invention opens another question.
The journey moves from the workshops of the pharaohs to the long, fragile work
of copying, comparing, losing, and rediscovering knowledge.

## An invitation, not a claim

This is a **counterfactual thought experiment**, not evidence that the ancient
Egyptians built telescopes or measured the speed of light. Attested people,
places, and objects meet established physics—and a deliberately invented chain
of events. The distinctions matter. So does the freedom to ask *what if?*

Five acts and twenty-five short scenes carry the story. Maps, timelines, and
optional numerical notes invite a closer look without turning the journey into
a technical manual.
